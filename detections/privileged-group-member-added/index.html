member added to a rarely-audited privileged group

ATT&CK · T1098 (Account Manipulation) Tactic · Persistence Tactic · Privilege Escalation
Hypothesis. Everyone alerts on additions to Domain Admins. Far fewer watch the privilege-adjacent groups, the built-in operators, DnsAdmins, Cert Publishers, Group Policy Creator Owners, the Remote Desktop and Remote Management users, Distributed COM Users, and the Key Admins. Each grants a durable capability, and each is a favoured backdoor precisely because the change is rarely alerted on.

context

This rule fills the blind spot beside the obvious one. An operator who cannot touch Domain Admins without setting off every alarm can often add themselves to a group that grants almost as much and watches almost nothing. DnsAdmins can load a DLL into the DNS service. Backup Operators can read the whole disk. The Key Admins reach the credential material.

It reads the group-membership events, 4728, 4732, and 4756, against a watchlist of those groups by SID and by name. Additions made by a machine account are excluded, because just-in-time tooling writes those as the computer account, and the loud groups are left to the rules that already cover them.

hunt

Match a member added to a watched privileged group by a human account.

// hypothesis: a human account adding a member to a watched privileged group.
// schema:     Windows Security event log (4728 / 4732 / 4756)

FROM logs-windows-security
| WHERE event.code IN (4728, 4732, 4756)
| WHERE winlog.event_data.TargetUserName IN ("DnsAdmins", "Backup Operators", "Account Operators", "Server Operators", "Key Admins")
| WHERE NOT ENDS_WITH(winlog.event_data.SubjectUserName, "$")
| KEEP host.name, winlog.event_data.SubjectUserName, winlog.event_data.TargetUserName, winlog.event_data.MemberSid
| SORT @timestamp DESC
| LIMIT 100

findings & discriminators

  • The quiet groups are the point. These grant durable capability and are rarely alerted on, which is exactly why an operator reaches for them instead of Domain Admins.
  • A machine subject is noise. Just-in-time tooling writes these adds as the computer account, so excluding a subject ending in a dollar sign leaves the human-driven changes.
  • Tie it to a change record. A human subject with no business reason and no ticket is the finding.

resulting sigma rule

Rule. Detects a member being added to a privileged or privilege-adjacent Windows group that sits outside the usual Domain Admins / Enterprise Admins watchlist: the built-in operator groups, DnsAdmins, Cert Publishers, Group Policy Creator Owners, Remote Desktop / Remote Management Users, Distributed COM Users, and the Key Admins.
