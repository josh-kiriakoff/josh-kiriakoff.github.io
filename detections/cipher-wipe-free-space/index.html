cipher.exe wiping free disk space

ATT&CK · T1070.004 (File Deletion) Tactic · Defense Evasion Tactic · Impact
Hypothesis. cipher.exe /w overwrites the free space on a volume so previously deleted file content can no longer be carved back. It is signed and built in, which makes it an attractive anti-forensic and anti-recovery tool, and the /w switch is distinct from the routine /e and /d encrypt operations.

context

The switch is the whole story. Everyday cipher use encrypts or decrypts files, and that is /e and /d. The wipe verb has one effect an intruder wants, which is to make deleted artefacts unrecoverable, so a defender can no longer carve back what was removed.

Because the binary is trusted, name-based and signature-based controls see nothing. The command line is the only place the intent shows.

hunt

Match cipher.exe invoked with the wipe switch.

// hypothesis: cipher.exe /w wiping free space.
// schema:     Huntress Process Insights - process_events

FROM logs-edr-process_events
| WHERE TO_LOWER(process.name) == "cipher.exe"
| WHERE TO_LOWER(process.command_line) LIKE "*/w*"
| KEEP host.name, user.name, process.parent.name, process.command_line
| SORT @timestamp DESC
| LIMIT 100

findings & discriminators

  • Separate the wipe from the encrypt. Scope to /w and the routine encrypt and decrypt traffic drops out. What is left is the anti-recovery use.
  • Read it in timeline. A free-space wipe next to deletions or a ransom event is a cleanup step, not a privacy habit.
