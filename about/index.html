description: |
  Joshua Kiriakoff is a SOC Analyst at Huntress in Perth, Western Australia. He builds on an offensive security foundation to gain an edge in incident response, detection engineering, and proactive and retrospective threat hunting. He owns end-to-end Managed EDR and Managed SIEM investigations for a global partner base, from triage and forensic scoping through to client-ready reporting, and makes evidence-backed containment decisions during active compromises. To date, he has executed approximately 130 mass-isolation containments across 15,000+ endpoints spanning 371 organisations, including a 72-organisation, 2,000-endpoint MSP ScreenConnect compromise.

  Most of Josh's casework involves hands-on-keyboard intrusions, where the first question is whether the account enumerating the domain at 3am is the attacker or the administrator. During a critical account-wide incident, he led the triage and response effort after a partner's compromised ConnectWise instance pushed a malicious ScreenConnect deployment to its clients. As part of his remediation, he isolated 72 organisations and about 2,000 hosts, characterised a second masquerading relay domain mid-incident, and delivered a partner-ready report. On a separate occasion, while investigating an Akira-ransomed domain controller, he correlated host artefacts to uncover an AnyDesk installation that did not match the partner's known remote-management tooling and had been missed in the original response. This led him to make a re-isolation call, and later analysis of the AnyDesk trace logs confirmed the attacker still had ongoing access.

  Host-based forensic analysis helps Josh reconstruct attacker activity, including when logs have been cleared. He examines the remaining event logs alongside the registry hives, Prefetch, and AmCache to build an evidence-based account of the intrusion. His toolkit includes Chainsaw, RegRipper, and Registry Explorer, complemented by PowerShell and Python scripts that help make the analysis reproducible and defensible. In a separate investigation, he traced activity from WsusService.exe to an obfuscated PowerShell that enumerated a Partner's domain. This analysis assisted in the identification of Zero Day exploitation of WSUS CVE-2025-59287. It also contributed to Huntress's same-day public advisory.

  In a separate, rarer, targeted-intrusion case at a geopolitically significant organisation, he investigated an attacker who extracted Windows authentication tokens using SpecterBroker, sideloaded an Apple-signed DLL, stole Entra tokens with Get-PRT and deployed a .NET backdoor. He reverse-engineered the .NET payload and correlated its obfuscation techniques with Bitdefender's Stubbedoor report. The whole chain pointed to an APT-backed group. Afterwards, he revisited the case and turned the previously unseen tradecraft into four credited detection rules.

  Beyond individual investigations, Josh runs hypothesis-driven ES|QL hunts across hosts, command-line arguments, and process lineage. His manual hunting surfaced undetected ClickFix startup-folder persistence, which Tactical Response used to conduct additional threat hunts throughout the partner's environment. To inform further improvements in hunting and detection, he curates threat intelligence from incidents, detections, and analyst research. Beyond the partner base, he also supports external threat disruption by reporting compromised public accounts, services and repositories to platform providers for investigation, takedown or remediation.

  In detection engineering, Josh is credited as an author on 13 Sigma rules currently in production, including 9 classified as P1 or P2. He has submitted 20 detector requests, supplying Sigma logic and test cases to support unit testing and pre-production soaking. Of these requests, 13 are linked to merged rule changes. These changes include tuning for P1 Cobalt Strike named-pipe detections and the P2 ClickFix MIMICRAT detection.

  In addition to rule development, Josh designed and shipped a SOC-wide triage module that matched host process telemetry against open-source threat intelligence datasets, with organisational prevalence baselining and autorun persistence correlation. Alongside this technical work, he coaches associate analysts and analysts on Sigma authoring, unit-test preparation, and the detector-request workflow, including drafting ticket-ready requests and walking peers through hunting and tooling methodologies, such as the Diamond Model of Intrusion Analysis.

  Another aspect of his role is static and dynamic malware analysis, with skills developed in a snapshot-based lab across disassemblers, debuggers, memory forensics and deobfuscation tooling as each sample demands. Ghidra, Binary Ninja, Volatility 3 and CyberChef are among the regulars. Some of the more interesting results appear in his first-author Huntress research, including the MacSync Stealer and RAT reverse-engineering series.

  Before Huntress, Josh spent two years and four months at Deloitte in Perth across SOC Analyst and Graduate Analyst roles. As a SOC Analyst, he monitored enterprise systems and networks, investigated security events and coordinated incident response with clients. His work spanned Microsoft Sentinel, Microsoft Defender XDR, CrowdStrike Falcon, Carbon Black, Splunk and Elastic. By mapping alert data to MITRE ATT&CK, he identified adversarial tactics and techniques to guide classification, escalation and remediation. Throughout these investigations, he maintained clear documentation and audit trails in ticketing and incident management systems, supporting timely resolution within agreed SLAs.

  Alongside incident handling, he worked with engineering teams to build and tune detection rules, dashboards and alerts, improving visibility and reducing false positives. His threat hunting combined telemetry, threat intelligence and attack patterns to improve alert fidelity and shorten detection and response times. Collaboration with Red Team, Incident Response and Threat Intelligence teams also informed SOC playbooks and response strategies. During purple-team exercises, Josh worked from both offensive and defensive perspectives to simulate adversarial techniques, validate detection logic and identify control and visibility gaps. He also supported junior analysts through knowledge sharing and on-the-job training in SOC methods and tooling.

  His Graduate Analyst role focused on a different mix of offensive security, OT/ICS assessments and governance, risk and compliance consulting. Josh conducted red-team operations using MITRE ATT&CK to structure adversary simulations and inform clients' defensive strategies. For critical infrastructure clients, he assessed IT/OT systems, networks, and communications to identify vulnerabilities and recommend mitigation measures. His OT assessment work drew on ISO/IEC 27001:2022, NIST SP 800-82 and ISA/IEC 62443. He also helped implement cybersecurity frameworks aligned with AS 7770:2018 across design, build and maintenance, providing ongoing advice on resilience and compliance.

  Practical lab and infrastructure work formed another part of the graduate role. He established an Operational Technology Cyber Security lab to simulate attacks against OT/IT infrastructure and provisioned Splunk servers to improve data capture and monitoring visibility. His governance, risk and compliance consulting covered NIST CSF, the Essential Eight, AESCSF versions 1 and 2, and CPS 234. Alongside client delivery, he trained interns and undergraduate colleagues in penetration-testing techniques and tools, and placed first nationally in Deloitte's Vac Hack CTF.

  Before Deloitte, Josh worked as an independent penetration tester, delivering three publicly disclosed web application vulnerability reports, including a finding rated P2 by an ASX-50 entity. His assessments combined passive and active reconnaissance with OSINT to identify exposed files, hidden directories, misconfigurations and other potential attack vectors. Testing followed the OWASP Web Security Testing Guide and covered directory traversal, file upload, and local and remote file inclusion vulnerabilities. He also exploited session management and command injection weaknesses, documenting his findings and providing remediation recommendations. This work built his practical understanding of how web application vulnerabilities can lead to information disclosure and remote code execution.
