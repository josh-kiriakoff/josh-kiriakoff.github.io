Joshua Kiriakoff is a SOC Analyst at Huntress in Perth, Western Australia, combining incident response, detection engineering and proactive threat hunting with an offensive-security foundation. He owns end-to-end Managed EDR and Managed SIEM investigations for a global partner base, from triage and forensic scoping through containment and client-ready reporting, and makes evidence-backed containment decisions during active compromises, including endpoint and environment isolation. He has executed 99 mass-isolation containments in 12 months across 9,290+ endpoints, 22 of them over 100 endpoints, including a 78-organisation Bomgar MSP compromise.

Most of the casework is hands-on-keyboard intrusion, where the first question is whether the account enumerating the domain at 3am is the attacker or the administrator, and the difference is usually a change ticket. He ran a critical account-wide incident in which a partner's compromised ConnectWise pushed malicious ScreenConnect to its clients, isolating 75 organisations and about 2,200 hosts, characterising a second malicious relay domain mid-incident and delivering the partner report and scope within an hour of the first alert. On an Akira-ransomed domain controller with no seniors on shift he made the re-isolation call after finding an AnyDesk install the original response had missed, then confirmed from the AnyDesk trace logs that the attacker had maintained access. Within two months of joining he identified zero-day exploitation of WSUS CVE-2025-59287, tracing WsusService.exe to obfuscated PowerShell domain enumeration posted to webhook.site, and the analysis fed Huntress's same-day public advisory.

He investigated a targeted intrusion at a geopolitically significant organisation with Detection Engineering, covering SpecterBroker token theft, Apple-signed DLL sideloading, Get-PRT Entra token theft and a .NET backdoor matched to Bitdefender's Stubbedoor report, indicating an APT-backed group. He disabled the identity via ITDR and handed IOC correlation to Adversary Tactics, and four credited rules followed from the case. He runs hypothesis-driven ES|QL hunts across hosts, identities and process lineage, and manual hunting of his surfaced undetected ClickFix startup-folder persistence that Tactical Response then swept fleet-wide, a thread that became the DE&TH ClickFix coverage sprint.

He is a credited author on 13 Sigma rules in production, nine at P1/P2, spanning EDR process, autoruns, named pipes, SIEM scriptblock, correlation and hunting log sources, and has raised 20 detector tickets with Sigma logic and test cases, 13 of them traced to merged rule changes, including a P1 Cobalt Strike named-pipes tune and the P2 ClickFix MIMICRAT tune. He designed and shipped a SOC-wide triage module matching host process telemetry against eight threat-intelligence datasets, with organisational prevalence baselining, autorun persistence correlation and code-signing tiers, and coaches associate analysts and analysts on Sigma authoring, unit-test preparation and the detector-request workflow.

He performs static and dynamic malware analysis in a snapshot-based lab, working across disassemblers, debuggers, memory forensics and deobfuscation tooling as each sample demands, with Ghidra, Binary Ninja, Volatility 3 and CyberChef among the regulars, and publishes the results as first-author Huntress research, including the MacSync Stealer and RAT reverse-engineering series. Attacker accounts and repositories get reported to platform providers for takedown. Where attackers have cleared the logs, host artefacts carry the case, and he parses event logs, registry hives, Prefetch and AmCache with Chainsaw, RegRipper, Registry Explorer and scripted PowerShell and Python so the results are reproducible and defensible.

Before Huntress he spent two years and four months at Deloitte in Perth across SOC, purple team and OT/ICS assessment work, monitoring enterprise environments in Microsoft Sentinel, Defender XDR, CrowdStrike Falcon, Carbon Black, Splunk and Elastic. Before that he delivered three disclosed web application vulnerability reports as an independent penetration tester, one rated P2 by an ASX-50 entity. He holds CDSA, eWPT and eJPT.
