Joshua Kiriakoff is a SOC Analyst at Huntress in Perth, Western Australia. He combines incident response, detection engineering and proactive threat hunting with an offensive-security foundation. Day to day he owns Managed EDR and Managed SIEM investigations for a global partner base end to end, from triage and forensic scoping through containment and client-ready reporting, and makes evidence-backed containment decisions during active compromises. Over the last 12 months that has added up to approximately 130 mass-isolation containments across 15,000+ endpoints in 371 organisations.

Most of Josh's casework involves hands-on-keyboard intrusions, where the first question is whether the account enumerating the domain at 3am is the attacker or the administrator. One of them was a critical account-wide incident at an MSP partner, whose compromised ConnectWise instance pushed a malicious ScreenConnect installer to its clients. He isolated 72 organisations and about 2,000 hosts, and while the incident was still running he characterised a second malicious relay domain. The partner had its report and scope within an hour of the first alert.

Other cases come down to a single artefact. On an Akira-ransomed domain controller, he correlated the evidence on the host against the partner's own remote-management tooling and found an AnyDesk install that matched none of it. The original response had missed it, and on that evidence he made the re-isolation call, then confirmed from the AnyDesk trace logs that the attacker had maintained access. In another, he traced WsusService.exe to obfuscated PowerShell that enumerated the domain and posted the result to webhook.site. That analysis helped identify zero-day exploitation of WSUS CVE-2025-59287 and fed Huntress's same-day public advisory. The evidence is often damaged by the time he gets it. Where attackers have cleared the logs, the artefacts left on the host have to carry the case, so he runs Chainsaw over whatever exported event logs remain, parses registry hives with RegRipper and Registry Explorer, and reads execution evidence out of Prefetch and AmCache when the binary itself is gone. The parsing is scripted in PowerShell and Python so the results are reproducible and defensible.

Targeted intrusions are rare, but Josh investigated one at a geopolitically significant organisation where the attacker extracted Windows authentication tokens using SpecterBroker, sideloaded an Apple-signed DLL, stole Entra tokens with Get-PRT and deployed a .NET backdoor. He reverse engineered the .NET payload and correlated its obfuscation techniques with Bitdefender's Stubbedoor report, and the whole chain pointed to an APT-backed group. Afterwards he went back to the case and turned the previously unseen tradecraft into 4 credited detection rules.

When the queue is quiet, he runs hypothesis-driven ES|QL hunts across hosts, identities and process lineage, looking for behaviour nothing currently catches. The hypotheses come from the threat intelligence he curates from incidents, detections and analyst research, and from TTPs observed in his own cases. One hunt turned up undetected ClickFix startup-folder persistence, which Tactical Response then swept from the fleet. His ScreenConnect hunting script, which classifies deployments over the Elastic API by relay-domain age and VirusTotal verdict, surfaced 15 malicious instances on its first sweep and seeded a hunting rule the same day. Beyond the partner base, he also reports compromised public accounts, services and repositories to platform providers for investigation, takedown or remediation.

On the detection side, he has been a credited author on 13 Sigma rules in production since January 2026, 9 at P1/P2, all submitted from the SOC through the Detection Engineering and Threat Hunting (DE&TH) review. A rule does not go to review until he has validated it himself. That means running unit tests over expanded positive and negative test data, passing the schema and lint checks, confirming the rule matches the same way the production engine will, and defining the false-positive sweep over production telemetry that has to come back clean before merge. Merged rules then soak at low severity on live data and are promoted once fidelity and false-positive rate hold up. One autoruns rule went to P2 six days after merge on a zero-false-positive soak. He has also raised 20 detector tickets with Sigma logic and test cases attached. Among these, 13 traced to merged rule changes, including a P1 Cobalt Strike named-pipes tune and the P2 ClickFix MIMICRAT tune.

In addition to the rules, Josh designed and shipped a SOC-wide triage module that matches host process telemetry against 8 open-source threat-intelligence datasets, 100,000+ rules in total, with organisational prevalence baselining, autorun persistence correlation and code-signing tiers. Alongside his own submissions, he coaches associate analysts and analysts through the same pipeline: Sigma authoring, unit-test preparation and the detector-request workflow, down to drafting a ticket-ready request. He also walks peers through hunt and tooling methodology such as the Diamond Model of Intrusion Analysis.

Malware analysis is another part of the job he has built skills in, mostly on the ransomware and infostealer families his own investigations surface. Samples go through static and dynamic analysis in a snapshot-based lab, across disassemblers, debuggers, memory forensics and deobfuscation tooling as each one demands, with Ghidra, Binary Ninja, Volatility 3 and CyberChef among the regulars. Some of the more interesting results go out as first-author Huntress research, including the MacSync Stealer and RAT reverse-engineering series.

Before Huntress, he spent two years and four months at Deloitte in Perth across SOC, purple team and OT/ICS assessment work, monitoring enterprise environments in Microsoft Sentinel, Defender XDR, CrowdStrike Falcon, Carbon Black, Splunk and Elastic. Earlier still, he delivered three disclosed web application vulnerability reports as an independent penetration tester, one rated P2 by an ASX-50 entity. He holds CDSA, eWPT and eJPT.
