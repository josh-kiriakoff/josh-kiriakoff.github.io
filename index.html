Joshua Kiriakoff is a SOC Analyst at Huntress, based in Perth, Western Australia. He manages the Managed EDR and Managed SIEM alert queues for a global partner base and carries each investigation end to end, from the initial signal through scoping, forensic analysis, remediation guidance, and the client-ready report. Signals are triaged through process ancestry and command-line analysis, scoped across every host in a tenant with ES|QL queries in Elastic, mapped to the MITRE ATT&CK framework, and enriched with open-source intelligence on attacker infrastructure.

Much of the casework involves hands-on-keyboard intrusions where the central problem is separating attacker activity from legitimate administration. ClickFix chains and rogue remote management tools are investigated as initial access, and attacker tunnelling over Cloudflared, ngrok, Chisel, and OpenSSH reverse tunnels is prioritised because it survives credential resets. Credential access against LSASS and NTDS.DIT is traced into attributed lateral movement over Impacket tooling, WMI, SMB, and RDP, and ransomware precursors such as shadow copy deletion, event log clearing, and Defender tampering are escalated ahead of encryption. Recurring tradecraft feeds detection coverage, and Akira intrusions repeatedly staging Active Directory enumeration output to C:\ProgramData\Adusers.txt are a classic example.

He performs deep-dive malware analysis of the ransomware and infostealer families encountered in his investigations, operating an isolated lab built on a custom-adapted FLARE-VM image. Analysis pairs Volatility 3 memory forensics and Ghidra static analysis with CyberChef deobfuscation of staged loaders and encoded payloads. Host artifacts carry investigations where telemetry ends and attackers have cleared logs, with Windows event logs parsed through Chainsaw, registry hives through RegRipper and Registry Explorer, and Prefetch and AmCache establishing execution after binaries are deleted.

During periods of low queue volume he conducts hypothesis-driven threat hunting in ES|QL, surfacing detection gaps where attacker behaviour is not yet covered. New detection logic is authored in Sigma and progresses through a structured lifecycle, tested for effectiveness and scope containment, deployed at low severity to support live investigations, and promoted to high-severity production detections as fidelity and false positive rates are proven. Detections already in production are held to the same standard and are tuned or demoted when they underperform or generate excessive false positive volume.

His prior experience includes SOC operations at Deloitte across Microsoft Sentinel, Defender XDR, CrowdStrike Falcon, Carbon Black, Splunk, and Elastic, alongside purple team exercises and OT/ICS security assessments. As an independent penetration tester he delivered three disclosed web application vulnerability reports to separate organisations, with one classified as a P2 by an ASX-50 entity.
