Joshua Kiriakoff is a SOC Analyst at Huntress in Perth, Western Australia. He works the Managed EDR and Managed SIEM queues for a global partner base and owns each investigation from first signal to final report. A signal gets judged on process ancestry and command line, scoped across every host in the tenant with ES|QL in Elastic, mapped to MITRE ATT&CK and enriched with open-source intelligence on the attacker's infrastructure. In 12 months that has meant 99 mass-isolation containments across more than 9,290 endpoints, including a Bomgar MSP compromise that reached 78 organisations and a ConnectWise supply-chain incident where he isolated 75 organisations and about 2,200 hosts and had the partner report out within an hour of the first alert.

Most of the casework is hands-on-keyboard intrusion, and the hard part is telling the attacker from the administrator. ClickFix chains and rogue remote management tools are worked as initial access. Tunnelling over Cloudflared, ngrok, Chisel and OpenSSH reverse tunnels goes to the top of the list because it survives a credential reset. Credential theft against LSASS and NTDS.DIT is followed into lateral movement over Impacket, WMI, SMB and RDP and pinned to a host, account and window, and ransomware precursors such as shadow copy deletion, log clearing and Defender tampering are escalated before encryption starts. Two months into the role he identified zero-day exploitation of WSUS CVE-2025-59287, and the analysis went into Huntress's public advisory the same day.

He reverses the ransomware and infostealer families his own cases turn up, in an isolated lab built on a custom FLARE-VM image, pairing Volatility 3 memory forensics with Ghidra and Binary Ninja static analysis and CyberChef for the staged loaders and encoded payloads. Where attackers have cleared the logs, host artefacts carry the case: Chainsaw over exported event logs, RegRipper and Registry Explorer over the hives, and Prefetch and AmCache to prove execution after the binary is gone.

When the queue is quiet he does detection engineering. He hunts in ES|QL for behaviour nothing currently catches and writes the fix in Sigma. Since January 2026 he has been credited on 13 production Sigma rules, nine at P1 or P2, raised 20 detector tickets of which 13 ended in merged rule changes, and shipped a SOC-wide triage module that matches process telemetry against eight threat-intelligence datasets with an Aho-Corasick engine over 80,000 patterns.

Before Huntress he spent two years and four months in Deloitte's SOC across Microsoft Sentinel, Defender XDR, CrowdStrike Falcon, Carbon Black, Splunk and Elastic, ran purple team exercises and did OT/ICS security assessments. As an independent penetration tester he delivered three disclosed web application reports to three organisations, one rated P2 by an ASX-50 company.
